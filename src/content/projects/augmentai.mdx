## Intro

AugmentAI generates synthetic images to bulk up small training datasets. If you don't have enough data, your model doesn't generalize well - AugmentAI uses GANs to fill that gap. I built it for the Dell x NVIDIA HackAI Hackathon, where it won an **Honorable Mention**.

## Problem

Good datasets are expensive to collect and label, especially for smaller projects. Without enough data, models tend to overfit or just underperform once they hit the real world.

## Solution

AugmentAI uses GANs to generate synthetic images and expand a dataset, on top of standard augmentation like rotation, flipping, and color jitter. It's hooked into NVIDIA AI Workbench for training, with a simple web interface - upload your images, get back augmented and synthetic versions.

## Project Overview

At its core, AugmentAI is a GAN model wrapped in a web app. Upload images, get back both augmented and fully synthetic outputs you can drop into a training pipeline.

### Key Features of AugmentAI

- Stability AI API for high-resolution synthetic image generation
- Simple upload-and-go web interface
- Rotation, flipping, and color adjustments for extra dataset variety
- Runs on NVIDIA AI Workbench so it scales to larger datasets

### Current Capabilities and Limitations

Right now the fallback local GAN tops out at 64x64 images - I'd like to push that resolution up and eventually support other data types like text or tabular data.

## Initial Phase (Using GANs)

### The Local GAN Approach

I started by writing a custom GAN, convolutional generator and discriminator, and training it locally on my M2 MacBook Air's CPU. Not exactly built for this - even with batch norm and Leaky ReLU to keep training stable, I was capped at 64x64 images and training took a while.

<MediaContainer src="/projects/augmentai/local-gan-approach.png" alt="AugmentAI local GAN approach" />

### Data Preparation and Augmentation

I trained on a set of nature photos, resized and normalized, with flipping, rotation, and color shifts applied to add variety before anything hit the model.

### Model Training and Metrics

I trained on 900 nature photos from a Kaggle dataset and tracked discriminator and generator loss to see whether the model was actually converging. The losses balanced out fine, but resolution was still stuck low because of the CPU setup.

<MediaContainer src="/projects/augmentai/training-metrics.png" alt="AugmentAI training metrics" />

## Second Phase (Using APIs)

### Transition to Stability AI API

To get past the resolution ceiling, I swapped in the Stability AI API. That let AugmentAI keep original image dimensions and cut generation time way down - it now handles most of the synthetic image work.

### Web Application Integration

The Flask backend handles uploads, augmentation, and serving results. It originally ran through my local GAN; now it calls the Stability AI API instead, which made the whole thing noticeably snappier for users.

## Final Designs

I kept the interface simple on purpose - upload, augment, done.

### Web Interface
