There's a lot of noise around AI products right now, and I was skeptical of most of it for a while - another chatbot wrapper, another "AI-powered" feature bolted onto an app that didn't need one. What's actually gotten me interested is a different shift: AI moving from novelty to infrastructure, quietly doing real work inside tools people already use instead of trying to be the whole product.

## What actually interests me

A few things stand out to me. Agents that can finish a task, not just answer a question, are a much more interesting problem - it's "can it plan, use tools, and recover when something breaks," not "can it respond." I've also seen more real productivity gains from AI baked into developer tools than from anything consumer-facing. And not every problem needs a giant general-purpose model - a small, well-scoped model doing one thing reliably often just works better.

## Building AugmentAI

That's a big part of why the [Dell x NVIDIA HackAI Hackathon](/#projects) was so fun to build for. My team built AugmentAI around a real, unglamorous problem instead of chasing a flashy demo, and it ended up earning an Honorable Mention. That project changed how I think about AI products - the goal wasn't to make the AI visible, it was to make the workflow around it disappear.

That mindset carried into my internship at Houlihan Lokey too, where a lot of the interesting work is figuring out how to apply AI to data engineering problems without turning it into a black box nobody trusts.

## Where I want to keep going

I don't think my job is to chase every new model release. It's figuring out where AI actually removes friction versus where it's just noise, and building the boring, solid infrastructure around it so it holds up in production. That's the work I want to keep doing after graduation, and it's a big part of why I'm looking for full-time roles in this space.
