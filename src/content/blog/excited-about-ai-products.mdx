There's a lot of noise around AI products right now, and I'll admit I was skeptical of a good chunk of it - another chatbot wrapper, another "AI-powered" feature bolted onto an existing app. What's actually gotten me excited is the shift from AI as a novelty to AI as infrastructure: models quietly doing real work inside tools people already use, instead of being the whole product.

## What actually excites me

- **Agents that finish tasks, not just answer questions.** The interesting problems aren't "can it respond," they're "can it plan, use tools, and recover when something goes wrong."
- **AI baked into developer tools.** Less flashy than consumer AI, but it's where I've seen the most genuine productivity gains firsthand.
- **Small, well-scoped models doing one thing reliably.** Not everything needs a giant general-purpose model - sometimes a focused model on a focused problem just works better.

## Building AugmentAI

This is a big part of why the [Dell x NVIDIA HackAI Hackathon](/#projects) was such a fun one to build for. My team built AugmentAI to tackle a real, unglamorous problem instead of chasing a flashy demo, and it ended up earning an Honorable Mention. That project changed how I think about "AI products" - the goal wasn't to make the AI visible, it was to make the workflow around it disappear.

That mindset carried over into my internship at Houlihan Lokey too, where a lot of the interesting work is figuring out how to apply AI to data engineering problems without turning it into a black box nobody trusts.

## How I want to keep contributing

I don't think my role is to chase every new model release - it's to figure out where AI genuinely removes friction versus where it's just noise, and build the boring-but-solid infrastructure around it so it actually holds up in production. That's the kind of work I want to keep doing after graduation, and honestly, it's a big part of why I'm looking for full-time roles in this space.
