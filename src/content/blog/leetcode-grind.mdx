I used to treat LeetCode like a chore - open a random problem, stare at it, get frustrated, close the tab. A few months into interview prep that's completely flipped, so I wanted to write down what changed.

## Patterns over problems

The biggest shift was realizing I wasn't trying to memorize solutions, I was trying to recognize patterns. Once I started grouping problems by technique instead of grinding random ones off a list, things clicked a lot faster - two pointers or sliding window for anything involving subarrays or substrings, BFS/DFS the moment something smells like a graph or a grid, DP when a problem asks for an optimal count, max, or min over overlapping subproblems. Once I could label a problem before I even finished reading it, the actual coding got a lot less stressful.

## What's helped most

Doing fewer problems but redoing them made a bigger difference than I expected - solving something once doesn't mean it's actually in long-term memory, and revisiting it cold a week later tells you a lot more. I also stopped obsessing over timing myself. Now I just make sure I can explain my approach out loud before I touch the keyboard. And I started keeping a running list of what trips me up - off-by-one errors, forgetting edge cases with empty inputs, messing up recursion base cases - and just having that list made those mistakes show up a lot less.

## Fitting it around USC and everything else

Between coursework, my internship, and side projects, I don't have hours a day for this, so consistency matters more than volume. A short, focused session most days beats one exhausting cram session a week, at least for me.

I still don't love every minute of it, but I've started actually enjoying the moment a hard problem clicks. Never thought I'd say that about LeetCode.
