I used to treat LeetCode like a chore - open a random problem, stare at it, get frustrated, close the tab. A few months into interview prep, my relationship with it has completely flipped, so I wanted to write down what actually changed.

## Patterns over problems

The biggest shift was realizing I wasn't actually trying to memorize solutions - I was trying to recognize patterns. Once I started grouping problems by technique instead of grinding random ones off a list, things clicked a lot faster:

- **Two pointers / sliding window** for anything involving subarrays or substrings
- **BFS/DFS** the moment a problem smells like a graph or a grid
- **DP** when a problem asks for an optimal count, max, or min over overlapping subproblems

Once I could label a problem before I even finished reading it, the actual coding got a lot less stressful.

## What's helped most

- **Doing fewer problems, but redoing them.** Solving something once doesn't mean it's in long-term memory. Revisiting a problem a week later, cold, tells you a lot more than grinding a new one.
- **Timing myself, but not obsessing over it.** Early on I was way too focused on hitting some arbitrary time. Now I just make sure I can explain my approach out loud before I touch the keyboard.
- **Keeping a running list of "what tripped me up."** Off-by-one errors, forgetting edge cases with empty inputs, messing up recursion base cases - writing these down made them show up way less often.

## Fitting it around USC and everything else

Between coursework, my internship, and side projects, I don't have hours a day to dedicate to this, so consistency matters more than volume. A short, focused session most days beats an exhausting cram session once a week - at least for me.

I'm not going to pretend I love every minute of it, but I've genuinely started enjoying the moment a hard problem clicks. That's a weird sentence to write, but here we are.
